If Approved Content Only is missing, first check that you are using the YouTube Kids mobile app and the intended child profile. Google requires that app to switch into Approve content yourself. Family Center, a TV menu and ordinary YouTube are not interchangeable with it. If the mode exists but the library looks wrong, inspect channel and collection approvals before assuming the restriction failed.

## Identify the account and the app

A YouTube Kids profile lives within a parent's setup. A child's managed Google account is a different account arrangement. Note which one the child uses and compare it with the current official instructions.

Open the YouTube Kids app, enter the parent settings and select the profile. Look in its content settings for Approve content yourself. A menu in normal YouTube may offer content levels without this particular approval control.

Do not change the child's birth date or remove supervision to imitate someone else's screenshot. Those changes have consequences outside video selection.

## If the menu is still absent

Update the app and confirm the signed-in parent account. Check whether the profile is the one linked to that account and whether the feature is supported for the account configuration and region.

Avoid repeatedly reinstalling the app before recording this information. Reinstallation does not change the underlying account relationship.

The [setup guide](/guides/youtube-kids-approved-content-only) describes the intended sequence. If the documented option remains absent, contact YouTube support with the app version and profile type, excluding private passwords or the child's personal details.

## If unexpected videos appear

Inspect the approvals. An approved channel can make more videos available than the one you checked. A collection can include several channels. Removing one individual approval may not remove access provided through a broader approval.

Then verify the actual child profile on the viewing device. A sibling profile, guest session or adult account can make a working restriction look ineffective.

Check shared content as well. A caregiver may have deliberately shared something into the child's experience.

## If the library is empty

Confirm that approvals were saved and the correct profile was selected. Try one eligible, ordinary video you can already play in the parent experience. Avoid using a live stream or Short as the first diagnostic example.

For content added from regular YouTube, [sharing has eligibility rules](/guides/share-youtube-video-with-child). Some videos cannot be shared even if the adult can watch them. A deleted, private or regionally unavailable video is another kind of problem.

## When a different setup is reasonable

If this configuration cannot support the specific videos or device your family uses, compare another selected-video player or parent-led viewing. The [alternatives guide](/guides/youtube-kids-alternatives) explains those choices.

LittleLoop is one option on iPhone and iPad for a parent-prepared list, but it still relies on YouTube's official embedded playback and can show platform ads. It is not affiliated with Google or YouTube, and it does not guarantee that every unavailable YouTube item becomes playable.

The aim of troubleshooting is to find the exact mismatch: account, menu, approval scope or video eligibility. Once you know that, the next step becomes much less speculative.
