If you want an app to control what your kid watches, first decide whether you need to choose individual videos, approve whole channels or simply limit viewing time. These are different jobs. YouTube Kids with Approved Content Only already handles the first two without a paid third-party player. Family Link and Apple Screen Time handle device access and time, but do not turn ordinary YouTube into a list of videos you approved. A separate player is worth considering when its approval workflow is easier for your family to maintain.

## Choose the kind of control first

Imagine your child asks to watch a particular science experiment. An age filter asks whether the video belongs in an allowed category. A channel allowlist asks whether its creator is approved. An individual-video allowlist asks whether you chose that exact upload. A time limit asks none of those questions. It just controls how long the app stays available.

| What you want | The control to look for | What still needs attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only the clips you chose | Individual-video approval | Someone must review and add each clip |
| Everything from selected creators | Channel approval | Future uploads may appear without another review |
| A broader age-filtered catalog | Supervised account or kids profile | Filtering can make mistakes |
| A daily stopping time | App or device limit | The limit does not judge the videos |
| A record of viewing | Watch history | A record is not prevention and can be incomplete |

For a broader decision tree, start with [every realistic way to control YouTube viewing](/guides/control-what-child-watches-on-youtube).

## Start with the controls you already have

YouTube Kids is a reasonable first trial when you want parent approval. Its Approve content yourself setting lets you choose videos, channels and collections. Search is unavailable in that mode. Approving a whole channel delegates more judgment than choosing one video, so start small. This costs your review time even when the app costs nothing.

Family Link is useful for a supervised Google account and supported device limits. It is not a universal remote control for every app on an iPad or television. Apple Screen Time handles a different part of the job on Apple devices. Guided Access can hold an iPhone or iPad inside one app during a session; it cannot approve the thumbnails inside that app.

You might need only one of these controls. Buying a new player will not solve an unlocked adult YouTube account sitting beside it.

## Compare parent-approved players

The descriptions below come from the providers' public documentation, not a hands-on security audit. Store availability, subscription terms and controls can differ by country and device. We do not rank these apps by untested safety claims.

### WhitelistVideo

[WhitelistVideo](https://whitelist.video/) describes a channel allowlist across desktop and mobile setups. That may suit a child who still needs YouTube for school while access to unapproved channels stays restricted. Check its installation instructions for every device, especially managed school computers. Its optional AI approval workflow is a different decision model from a parent reviewing each video. Decide whether you want that automation before enabling it.

### SafeTube by AstroSafe

[SafeTube](https://www.playsafetube.com/) describes a child-facing library, approved channels, profiles, viewing history and time controls. It may appeal to families who want help finding content as well as restricting it. Ask whether an approved channel exposes future uploads and which controls work on your child's device. Several products use similar names; this comparison refers specifically to playsafetube.com.

### Tubsy

[Tubsy](https://tubsy.app/) describes video, playlist and channel approval, with web, Android and Android TV access. Its channel subscriptions can add new uploads to the child's feed automatically. That saves work but is not individual review. Its FAQ also acknowledges that the YouTube player may show ads, so do not interpret a cleaner surrounding interface as an unconditional ad-free promise.

### Kidube

[Kidube](https://kidube.com/) combines its own catalog with content parents can assign, including external video. The distinction matters for advertising. The provider describes its own hosted catalog as ad-free, while external embeds may carry ads. Check which type of video your child will actually watch and how access works on your device before choosing a subscription.

### Kivvie

[Kivvie](https://kivvie.app/) describes a parent web dashboard and child apps built around approved YouTube channels. This is a channel-first option, rather than a reason to assume every future upload has been individually reviewed. Check profile and channel limits on the current plan, and test the parent workflow on the phone you normally carry.

### Vidling

[Vidling](https://www.vidling.app/) describes approval for videos, playlists and channels, with AI-assisted suggestions and a final parent decision. It says playback uses official YouTube embeds. Review the recurring subscription interval carefully: a weekly offer and an annual offer are not comparable by their headline price. Confirm current store availability and renewal terms before subscribing.

### Kovi and Cuppie

[Kovi](https://kovikids.com/) currently presents an upcoming iPhone and iPad product, including requests and remote approval. Treat that as a product to watch, not an available solution to today's viewing problem.

We also checked [Cuppie](https://www.cuppie.net/), but could not establish enough current public documentation to make a dependable feature or availability comparison. That is a limit of this review, not evidence that the product is unsuitable. Check its live listing and support information if you are considering it.

## Where LittleLoop fits

LittleLoop is the app our team makes. It is an iPhone and iPad option for families who want to assemble a small viewing list and hand over a child player without open search. Parents must choose the content first. That ongoing work is part of the product, not a task the app removes.

Playback uses YouTube's official embedded player, and YouTube-served ads may appear. LittleLoop is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google or YouTube. It is not an Android or smart TV solution, and it does not restrict separate apps or browsers on the device. If YouTube Kids approval already works well for you, there is no need to switch just to obtain an approval model.

## Test before you pay

Use a normal evening as the test. Add two acceptable videos and leave a third unapproved. Try opening the third through search, a link, a different app and another account. Then let the approved list finish. Check what happens next, whether ads appear and whether the child can leave the player. Do not test only the parent dashboard.

Ask how to revoke approval, whether whole-channel access includes tomorrow's uploads and what happens when a video disappears. Read the privacy policy for stored child profiles and history. Finally, make sure another caregiver can run the same setup without borrowing your password.

If this sounds like more maintenance than your family wants, compare [fixed video libraries and no-app alternatives](/guides/youtube-kids-alternatives). If you want to keep regular YouTube, the guide to [viewing without YouTube Kids](/guides/safe-youtube-without-youtube-kids) explains the compromises. The right choice is the one whose remaining gaps you understand and can manage.
