The most reliable way to control what your child watches on YouTube is to decide whether you want to filter a large catalog or allow a small list. YouTube Kids can do either: its standard age settings filter the catalog, while Approved Content Only limits the child to videos, channels and collections you select. Device tools such as Screen Time and Family Link control when the app can run, but they do not choose each video. If you want a hard allowlist, use Approved Content Only, an approved-only player, or play selected videos yourself on a shared screen.

## Start with the kind of control you need

Parents often reach for one setting and expect it to solve four different jobs. No single switch does.

| What you want | The control that matches |
| --- | --- |
| Stop one video rolling into the next | Disable autoplay |
| Prevent browsing across the catalog | Turn off search or use an allowlist |
| Keep out a known channel | Block that channel |
| Set a stopping time | Use the YouTube Kids timer or a device limit |
| Decide every video in advance | Use Approved Content Only or an approved-only player |

This distinction matters. A 30-minute app limit can stop a session on time while still allowing any video available inside the app. A carefully approved list can control content while letting the session run longer than you intended. Most families need one content rule and one time rule.

## Option 1: Tighten YouTube Kids

YouTube Kids is the sensible first stop for many younger children because it is free and already has parent controls. Before replacing it, check the settings that change the experience most:

1. Open the parent settings or the linked parent's Family Center.
2. Select the child's profile.
3. Turn on **Disable autoplay** so the child cannot turn autoplay back on.
4. Turn off **Search** if you do not want open-ended browsing.
5. For the narrowest catalog, choose **Approved Content Only** and add the videos or channels yourself.

These settings are not interchangeable. Turning off search still leaves videos selected by YouTube Kids available. Google's own documentation says its system uses automated filtering, user feedback and human review, and also says no system is perfect. Approved Content Only changes the model: the available set comes from your choices.

The tradeoff is setup time. Approving a whole channel is quick, but future uploads from that creator may not resemble the videos you checked. Approving one video at a time is slower and more predictable.

Follow the separate instructions for [disabling autoplay](/guides/stop-youtube-autoplay-for-kids) or [setting up Approved Content Only](/guides/youtube-kids-approved-content-only). The approval setting needs the YouTube Kids app, not just Family Center.

## Option 2: Limit the device

Apple Screen Time and Google Family Link are useful when the real problem is timing. They can schedule downtime, set app limits or block an app. Guided Access on an iPhone or iPad can also keep the device inside one app while a child is holding it.

None of those tools reviews the next thumbnail. An app limit controls access to the app, not which videos it contains.

Use a device control when:

- the child has their own phone or tablet;
- the same daily rule should cover several apps;
- bedtime or school hours matter more than a precise playlist;
- you need to keep a young child from leaving the current app.

Pair it with YouTube Kids content settings if you care about both time and selection.

## Option 3: Use an approved-only player

Approved-only players start from an empty library. A parent adds videos or channels, and the child sees only that smaller collection. Products in this category include SafeTube, WhitelistVideo and LittleLoop, but they do not all work the same way. Some approve channels, some individual videos, and some are designed for older children using regular YouTube.

Our [comparison of parent-control video apps](/guides/apps-to-control-what-kids-watch) gives the provider-specific differences.

Ask four questions before installing one:

- Can the child search or open recommendations?
- Does approval apply to a video, a playlist or an entire channel?
- Can the child leave the player without a parent code?
- Which devices does it support today?

LittleLoop is an option when a parent wants to approve individual YouTube videos and hand over an iPhone or iPad in a separate child mode. It also has child profiles, daily limits and shared caregiver access. It is a poor fit if you want to leave YouTube completely, need Android today, want offline downloads or do not want to prepare a list.

Videos in LittleLoop use YouTube's official embedded player. That means availability still depends on YouTube, and YouTube-served ads may appear. An approved-only player controls the surrounding experience. It does not take ownership of the video itself.

LittleLoop is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google or YouTube.

## Option 4: Use no extra app

A new app is not always the cleanest answer. For occasional viewing, a family can use a shared television or a parent's device and keep selection with the adult.

A workable no-app routine looks like this:

1. Agree on one video or one episode before pressing play.
2. Turn autoplay off.
3. Keep the remote or device with the adult.
4. Use a visible stopping point, such as the end of the chosen video.
5. If the child wants another video, treat it as a new choice rather than the default next step.

This costs nothing and requires no account setup. It also requires an adult to be nearby. It works well for a Saturday-morning video and poorly when several caregivers need to follow the same rules.

## A practical way to choose

Start with the lightest setup that answers the actual problem.

- If autoplay is the only issue, disable it in Family Center.
- If the child is young and YouTube Kids has the content you need, try Approved Content Only.
- If time is the issue, use Screen Time or Family Link.
- If the child should see a small, shared library on a handed-over device, compare approved-only players.
- If viewing is occasional, choose the video yourself and skip another app.

There is no universal best option. The right one is the least complicated setup that a family will keep using after the first week.
