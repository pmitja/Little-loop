On an iPhone or iPad, combine a suitable YouTube account with Apple controls for access and time. Use YouTube Kids or an eligible supervised YouTube account for content rules, Screen Time for recurring limits and Guided Access for a temporary handoff. None of these settings guarantees that every available video will suit your child. Choose the content boundary before setting the device timer.

## Whose device is it?

A child's own iPad and a parent's phone handed over for ten minutes need different setups. For the child's device, use Family Sharing and Screen Time to manage ongoing rules. For a brief session on your phone, Guided Access can keep the device in the current app.

Using your ordinary adult YouTube account is a separate choice. Device locking does not turn that account into a child experience.

## Set the content rule

If the child uses YouTube Kids, choose the profile's content settings. Approve content yourself is the narrowest option when you want to select the available videos. For a broader catalog, choose an appropriate content level and review search and autoplay.

For supervised regular YouTube, use the linked parent's Family Center controls. Check eligibility and the available content levels rather than assuming every child account has the same features.

The [overall control guide](/guides/control-what-child-watches-on-youtube) explains those choices before you commit to one.

## Set recurring access and time rules

Open Screen Time for the child in Apple's family settings. Review App Limits, Downtime and Content & Privacy Restrictions. Protect changes with a Screen Time passcode.

Set a limit for the video app and enable blocking at the limit where available. Check the browser route too. Restricting an app alone may leave the same service accessible on the web.

Apple's web restrictions can help control access to sites. They do not inspect every video inside an allowed streaming app. Use the [time-limit guide](/guides/youtube-time-limit-for-kids) if the daily allowance is the primary problem.

## For a temporary handoff

Open the chosen video app and start Guided Access after enabling it in Accessibility settings. You can restrict touch or parts of the screen and set a time limit. Practice ending the session yourself before handing over the phone.

The dedicated [Guided Access guide](/guides/lock-ipad-to-one-app-for-child) covers setup and version differences. It also explains why keeping a child inside an app still leaves the app's own buttons available unless you restrict them.

## Where LittleLoop fits

LittleLoop is an iPhone and iPad option for parents who want to prepare a small list of YouTube videos and use a separate child mode. Parents review the videos first; the app provides profiles, time limits and a parent PIN.

It does not lock the whole operating system or eliminate the need for device controls. Its official YouTube embed can show platform-served ads, and LittleLoop is not affiliated with Google or YouTube. It is also not a way to leave YouTube's infrastructure behind.

## Test one ordinary session

Start the same way the child normally starts. Confirm the correct profile, play a video, reach the time limit and check whether the child can leave the app. The useful result is a predictable everyday flow.

Keep a short note of which settings the other caregiver needs to know. A setup both adults can maintain is more useful than a complicated arrangement only one person understands.
