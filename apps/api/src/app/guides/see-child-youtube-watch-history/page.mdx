Review a child's YouTube history on the device and account they use for watching. For a supervised YouTube account, Google directs parents to the child's My Activity, YouTube History and Manage history. YouTube Kids has a recent-viewing area such as Watch it again. These records can help you talk about viewing, but they are not a complete account of attention, intent or everything watched elsewhere.

## Begin with the account

Sit with the child's usual device and confirm the profile. On a shared tablet it is easy to review an adult account while assuming it represents the child's activity.

For supervised YouTube, open the history management route associated with the child's account. Google's instructions describe My Activity, then YouTube History and Manage history. In YouTube Kids, use the child's recent-viewing area. Menu names and layout differ across devices.

Family Link helps manage controls, but do not assume it provides a remote, complete list of every watched video. The official guidance specifies the child's device for reviewing watch history.

## Read the record cautiously

An entry tells you the account opened a video. It does not tell you whether the child selected it, whether autoplay led there, how closely they watched or whether someone else was holding the device.

For example, an unexpected science clip might be a sibling's choice on the shared profile. A familiar cartoon entry might have played briefly while the child walked away. Ask about what happened before drawing conclusions.

Use history to spot practical questions: is one channel dominating the choices, are sessions starting in another app, or are recommendations leading away from the original topic?

## Why something may be missing

History can be paused, cleared or recorded under a different account. Signed-out viewing and activity in another service may not appear where you are looking.

If you are about to reset recommendations, inspect any activity you want to discuss first. [Clearing and pausing history](/guides/reset-youtube-kids-recommendations) changes what remains available for later review. Pausing history for privacy and expecting a full future viewing log are conflicting goals.

A missing entry should not automatically be treated as evidence that a child concealed something. Check the setup first.

## Use the information openly

Tell a child, in language appropriate to their age, what you check and why. "I want us to choose videos that work for you" is a more useful starting point than treating the history page as a disciplinary record.

For a younger child, review together and select a few favorites for next time. For an older child, discuss a surprising entry and agree what to do when unfamiliar material appears.

## When you need a different control

A history log is retrospective. It helps after viewing. If you want to decide the available videos before viewing, use an approval model described in the [control guide](/guides/control-what-child-watches-on-youtube).

Some [parental video apps](/guides/apps-to-control-what-kids-watch) include activity reporting, but check exactly what it records and on which devices. No app's report should be described as a record of the child's entire online life. Choose a log because it answers a specific family question, not because a longer report looks more reassuring.
