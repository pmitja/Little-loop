You can stop YouTube autoplay for a child by using the linked parent's account, opening Family Center, selecting the child and enabling Disable autoplay. That parental setting also prevents the child from switching autoplay back on. It is more useful than simply toggling the button beside a playing video. The setting applies to the child's supported experience, so first check that they are watching with the profile you manage.

## Set the parental control

On the parent's mobile YouTube app, open You, then Settings and Family Center. Choose the child's profile or account and turn Disable autoplay on. On a computer, open your profile picture, Settings, then Family Center and the option to manage children's profiles. Select the child before changing the control.

Family Link provides another route: choose the child, open Controls and YouTube, then look in the general settings. Labels can vary between versions. Use the linked parent account rather than an unrelated Google account on the same phone.

The wording is easy to misread. You are turning *Disable autoplay* on, which turns automatic next-video playback off.

## Check it on the device they actually use

Open the child's viewing profile and play a short video to its end. Do this outside a playlist first. Confirm that another recommended video does not start automatically and that the child cannot simply re-enable the autoplay control.

Then test any playlist you normally use. Sequential playlist playback and YouTube's recommended-next-video autoplay are different behaviors. A queue may still progress through its items, so an autoplay setting alone is not a promise that every possible session ends after one clip.

If a television or tablet behaves differently, check its signed-in profile. Watching as a guest, with an adult's account or through another player may not use the child's setting. Test each usual viewing route once.

## When autoplay seems to turn itself on

Start with the account, not a reinstall. Compare the account or profile shown on the watching device with the one selected in Family Center. A switch made on an adult's ordinary player is not the same as a parental restriction.

Next, distinguish a new full video from an animated thumbnail, a feed preview or the next item in a playlist. These can look like autoplay while being controlled elsewhere. Finally update the app and check the relevant official instructions for that device. Avoid promising a permanent fix from clearing a cache.

## What remains after autoplay is off

The child may still choose another thumbnail, search for something new or open a different app. Disabling autoplay adds a stopping point; it does not create a list of approved videos.

If the next-tap choices are the concern, read the guide to [unwanted recommendations](/guides/stop-youtube-recommendations-for-kids). If browsing itself is the problem, [turning off search](/guides/turn-off-youtube-kids-search) is a separate control. For the duration of the entire session, use a [time limit](/guides/youtube-time-limit-for-kids).

You do not need to install another app just to change autoplay. Make the parental setting, test the actual viewing setup and then decide whether anything else still needs fixing.
