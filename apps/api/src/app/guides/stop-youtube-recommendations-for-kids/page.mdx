To stop unwanted YouTube recommendations, first decide whether you want a better recommendation feed or no open feed at all. Clearing and pausing history changes the signals used for recommendations. Blocking a channel excludes that source. Neither creates a list in which every video has your approval. For that, use YouTube Kids with Approved Content Only or a separate player limited to selected videos.

## Why unwanted suggestions keep coming back

A recommendation system works from available signals and a catalog. Watching a video, repeating it or searching for a topic can influence later choices. One accidental viewing session may affect what appears next.

That does not mean every unfamiliar suggestion came from something your child watched. Recommendations can draw from more than one signal, and the platform can offer material outside your expectations. A clean history is not a guarantee of a particular kind of content.

Start by looking at the actual child's profile. Shared adult accounts can mix several people's interests, making the feed harder to interpret.

## If you want to keep recommendations

Review recent activity first. Then clear old watch and search history if it is no longer useful, and consider pausing collection of new history. The [reset guide](/guides/reset-youtube-kids-recommendations) gives the sequence and explains what gets lost.

Block a specific unwanted channel through the child's controls. This is more targeted than repeatedly dismissing individual suggestions. It still will not exclude copies of the same material uploaded elsewhere.

Change one thing at a time, then look at the child view. Otherwise it is hard to tell whether you changed the recommendations, the available catalog or simply the profile.

## If you want every choice to be yours

Use [Approved Content Only](/guides/youtube-kids-approved-content-only) in YouTube Kids. Start with individual videos if you do not want a creator's wider catalog included. Channel and collection approvals are broader decisions.

A separate approved-video player can also provide a smaller selection. Read its behavior carefully: some tools approve entire channels while others require individual review. "Curated" may mean a parent selected the material, an editor selected it or software scored it. Those are different arrangements.

Another practical option is a shared screen with an adult choosing the next video. This requires attention but avoids a new account or subscription.

## Autoplay is a separate decision

A feed can show suggestions without automatically playing them. Equally, a playlist can move to another selected video after the first finishes.

[Turn off autoplay](/guides/stop-youtube-autoplay-for-kids) if the immediate problem is continuous playback. Then decide what should be available to tap. Testing those two questions separately is easier than trying to infer everything from whether the screen stops moving.

## Do not promise a blank YouTube everywhere

Advice written for a desktop extension may not work in the mobile app or television app. Hiding one panel also does not necessarily block access to the underlying video.

For a family setup, the useful test is concrete: after the selected video finishes, what can the child press next on this device? If those choices are acceptable, the setup may already be sufficient. If they are not, changing the available library is usually more dependable than repeatedly retraining recommendations.
