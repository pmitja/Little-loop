Family Link can manage supported YouTube content settings, disable autoplay, control history settings and limit app or device time on supported child devices. It does not give parents a universal per-video approval switch for ordinary YouTube. Separate the Google account controls from Android and Chromebook device controls before choosing it as your family's main solution.

## Two kinds of control in one place

The YouTube account settings affect the supported child's viewing experience. Device settings determine whether an app is available and how long the device can be used.

A parent using Family Link on an iPhone can manage a supported child's Android device. That does not mean Family Link imposes Android-style device restrictions on a child's iPhone. The parent's phone and the supervised device have different roles.

## What it can change on YouTube

Select the child in Family Link, open Controls and then YouTube. The available choices depend on whether the child uses YouTube Kids or a supervised YouTube account.

For YouTube Kids, controls include content level and search access. General settings can include autoplay and history controls. Regular supervised YouTube uses broader content levels rather than a list of individually approved videos.

Choose the child's experience deliberately. The comparison of [YouTube Kids and supervised accounts](/guides/youtube-kids-vs-supervised-account) explains that transition.

## What the time controls cover

Family Link provides app limits, device daily limits and schedules on supported Android devices and Chromebooks. Some system apps cannot be limited. Apps marked as unlimited can behave differently during daily limits and schedules.

Do not assume a smart TV follows a phone's time budget. Check which devices are shown and supported. A YouTube account setting can travel with an account while an operating-system limit remains specific to supported devices.

The [Android guide](/guides/control-youtube-on-android) covers setup. For choosing between a one-off session and a daily allowance, use the [time-limit guide](/guides/youtube-time-limit-for-kids).

## What it cannot tell you

Family Link is not an independent review of every video. A content level remains a platform-selected catalog. Nor should app-usage totals be presented as a complete explanation of what a child watched or understood.

Google directs parents to the child's device to review YouTube watch history. If you need a particular reporting feature, check the current product documentation instead of assuming it exists because the app manages supervision.

## A good fit and a poor fit

Family Link is a strong starting point for a child with their own Android device when the family needs account supervision, app access and daily schedules. It is available without buying a separate video-player subscription.

It is less suited to the narrow rule "only these ten videos may play inside regular YouTube." YouTube Kids approval settings or a dedicated selected-video player may fit that requirement better.

Keep the decision tied to the job. A broad supervision tool and a small approved library can complement each other, but installing both only helps if each one solves a real gap.
