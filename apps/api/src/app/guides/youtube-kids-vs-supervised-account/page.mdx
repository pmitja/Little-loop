YouTube Kids offers a smaller child-focused experience, including a mode where a parent selects the available videos. A supervised YouTube account gives access to a broader version of YouTube with age-related content settings and parental controls. Moving between them changes both the catalog and the child's freedom to explore. It is not simply an upgrade to the same app.

## Compare the selection model

YouTube Kids uses age-based settings for its ordinary catalog. It also offers Approve content yourself in the app for supported profiles. That gives a parent direct control over the videos, channels and collections included.

Supervised YouTube offers broader content settings for children eligible for that experience. It does not turn normal YouTube into an empty library waiting for the parent's individual approvals.

| Question | YouTube Kids | Supervised YouTube |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the experience built around younger viewers? | Yes | A broader YouTube experience |
| Can parents choose a content level? | Yes | Yes |
| Is there a parent-selected library mode? | Approved Content Only for supported profiles | Not a universal per-video approval mode |
| Should parents review the setup? | Yes | Yes, especially after a transition |

## Why a family might consider moving

A child may want a tutorial, hobby channel or creator absent from YouTube Kids. Start by checking whether that particular video can be shared into the existing Kids profile. A request for one missing video does not automatically require access to the broader catalog.

If the child increasingly needs a wider range of material, a supervised account may fit better. Discuss what they should do when a title, thumbnail or video feels wrong, and review the settings together.

Eligibility and available experiences depend on age and country. Use the child's actual age and Google's current local requirements.

## Controls to revisit

Recheck autoplay, content level, history and blocked channels. Do not assume the new experience inherits every detail of the previous setup. Also inspect device time limits and browser access.

The [Family Link guide](/guides/youtube-family-link-parental-controls) distinguishes account controls from device restrictions. That distinction becomes more important when a child watches on several screens.

## If neither experience fits

Some families want a broader source catalog while keeping a short list of permitted videos. An approved-video player may support that arrangement, though availability and eligibility still depend on the provider.

Other families prefer to leave YouTube for a fixed streaming library. The [alternatives comparison](/guides/youtube-kids-alternatives) covers that decision. Shared-screen viewing is also reasonable when the wider catalog is needed only occasionally.

## Avoid treating age as the only decision

The meaningful questions are what the child needs to watch, how independently they will browse and how much ongoing review the adults can manage. Two children of the same age may use a screen in very different ways.

If you decide against YouTube Kids, the guide to [other YouTube setups](/guides/safe-youtube-without-youtube-kids) explains the remaining choices. Make a change because the current arrangement no longer fits, then test the new experience together before handing it over.
